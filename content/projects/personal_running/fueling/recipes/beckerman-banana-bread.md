# The Beckerman Banana Bread

A nostalgic banana bread recipe from Wisconsin Track Club race days. Sweet, simple, and best shared after a hard effort.

## Why This Recipe Matters

When I was running with Wisconsin Track Club at UW, meets were some of my favorite days. Part of that was the racing, part of it was the travel, and part of it was the little rituals that made the team feel like a real community.

One of those rituals was banana bread.

Alex Beckerman, or someone in his family, would bring banana bread for the team at meets. After racing, it was always a small joy to see the tin come out, unwrap the foil, and share slices with everyone. There was something generous and grounding about it: time, love, and effort baked into something simple, then passed around after everyone had just gone through some version of a hard race.

That memory stuck with me.

Maybe it tasted better because we were exhausted. Maybe food just hits differently after racing. But I remember feeling genuinely warmed by it. This banana bread reminds me that running is not just about fitness or results. It is also about community, shared effort, and the small ways people take care of each other.

Anytime I make it, I get taken back to that feeling.

## Ingredients

- 3–4 ripe bananas
  - use 3 if large
  - use 4 if average-sized
- 1/2 cup Greek yogurt
- 3/4 cup brown sugar
- 2 eggs
- 1/2 stick butter, melted
- 2 cups flour
- 2 tsp baking soda
- 1/2 to 3/4 cup mini chocolate chips

## Equipment

- Mixing bowl
- Fork or potato masher
- Measuring cups/spoons
- 3 greased mini loaf pans
- Toothpick
- Cooling rack or cool stovetop

## Directions

1. Preheat the oven to 350°F.
2. Grease 3 mini loaf pans.
3. Mash the bananas in a large mixing bowl.
4. Add the Greek yogurt and brown sugar. Mix until combined.
5. Add the eggs and mix.
6. Add the melted butter and mix again.
7. In a separate bowl, mix the flour and baking soda.
8. Slowly add the flour and baking soda mixture into the wet ingredients.
9. Mix until just combined. Do not overmix.
10. Add the mini chocolate chips and stir gently until just mixed.
11. Pour the batter evenly into the 3 greased mini loaf pans.
12. Bake at 350°F for about 30 minutes.
13. Test with a toothpick. The loaves are done when the toothpick comes out clean.
14. Cool on a rack or cool stovetop.
15. Slice after cooled.

## Notes

- The batter should be mixed gently once the flour is added.
- Mini chocolate chips work better than regular chocolate chips because they distribute more evenly.
- This is a good recipe to make for teammates, friends, or a post-run hangout.
- Best eaten after a run, race, or with coffee.

## Variations to Try

- Add cinnamon.
- Add walnuts or pecans.
- Use dark chocolate chips.
- Make one larger loaf instead of mini loaves, but adjust baking time and test carefully with a toothpick.
- Serve with peanut butter or Greek yogurt for a more filling snack.
